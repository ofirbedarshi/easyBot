# How the Builder works

Describes the Builder as implemented on 2026-10-24. Questioning is model-driven. The application has no fixed question list. Prompt text tells the model what to do; it does not guarantee the model will do it. Only the application checks below are enforced.

## What it does and produces

The Builder interviews the business owner in chat and fills a draft business profile. The first line is fixed application text: it frames the Builder as a new teammate learning how the business handles inquiries, invites the owner to say what they do and what they want the customer bot to achieve, and mentions a summary to approve and `/test`. Later replies come from the model (`OPENAI_MODEL`, otherwise `gpt-4o-mini`).

The draft can hold: business name, what the business does, **ownerGoal** (desired outcome for the customer bot), services (name, price, notes), hours, location or service area, common customer questions (FAQ), information the bot should collect from the customer (intake), explicit unknowns, and **conversation settings** (behavior — not business facts): **tone** (free text), **responseLength** (`short` | `standard` | null), **priceDisclosure** (`relevant` | `on_request` | null), and **assistant identity**: **assistantName** (free text | null) and **openingMessage** (owner's opening text | null). Unknown text fields are stored as null. `ownerGoal` stays null until the owner clarifies an outcome — the app never invents a goal. `commonQuestions` is an array of `{ question, answer }` objects: `question` is what customers ask; `answer` is the owner’s explanation the Business Bot may tell customers. Legacy bare strings are normalized on load/merge to `{ question, answer: "" }`. Only FAQ items with a non-empty `answer` are injected into the Flowise customer prompt as approved facts. `informationToCollect` uses a different convention: **null** means the owner has not been asked yet; **[]** means the owner said collect nothing; a non-empty array lists the items. Each turn saves that draft. Live approved fields change only when the model marks the turn approved and the minimum below is met. Approval writes the profile (with `approvedAt`) and syncs a Flowise chatflow whose customer prompt is built from those approved fields: approved business facts (identity, services, hours, location, FAQ answers), explicit unknowns, intake whitelist, `ownerGoal` as conversational direction only (not a customer-facing fact source), and — separately — conversation settings (tone, reply length, price disclosure). There is no approved language field; the Business Bot prompt tells the model to match the customer’s language as a behavior setting.

**Conversation settings defaults (enforced on read/use, not written into stored nulls):** when `tone` is null, effective tone is `friendly, clear, and professional`. When `responseLength` is null, effective value is `standard`. When `priceDisclosure` is null, effective value is `relevant`. Invalid enum spellings are normalized or dropped to null on load. Legacy profiles without the new keys load safely and keep any existing `tone`. When `assistantName` is null the Business Bot has no name (the app never invents one). When `openingMessage` is null the customer prompt tells the Business Bot to give a short introduction as the digital assistant of the approved business name (or "this business"). There is no fixed default opening text.

**Assistant identity (prompt, not enforced):** the customer prompt tells the Business Bot to say openly that it is a digital assistant and never to present itself as the owner or a human. It uses the opening only in its first reply of a **fresh conversation**, meaning a Flowise `sessionId` with no earlier turns in its Buffer Memory. Each `/test` starts a new `testSessionId`, and probes that pass their own session id are also fresh. If the first message is a real question, the Business Bot should open and answer it in the same reply, and should not re-introduce itself later. The app does not inject the opening; this is model behavior driven by chat history. The opening is customer-facing wording only. It cannot establish facts, prices, capabilities, or intake, and it does not override the price policy. Conflicting parts are to be left out.

**Explicit identity reset (enforced on merge):** an omitted, null, or blank `assistantName` / `openingMessage` in the draft preserves the live value. To clear one back to its default, the draft carries `assistantNameReset: true` / `openingMessageReset: true` with a null value. `reconcileDraft` keeps a pending reset flag across later turns until a new non-empty value replaces it. `mergeDraft` applies the reset on approval. The prompt tells the model when to set these flags (an owner asking to remove the name or restore the default opening); whether the model does so reliably is **unverified**.

**Price disclosure / reply-length labels (single source):** owner-facing wording for the enums lives in `PRICE_DISCLOSURE_LABELS` and `RESPONSE_LENGTH_LABELS` in `profile.js` (`relevant` → “when useful and relevant”; `on_request` → “only when the customer asks about pricing”; `short` / `standard` as themselves). `formatSummary` and the Builder system prompt both read those maps so the explanation, pending draft, and approval summary stay aligned. Customer-prompt policy text in `customerPrompt.js` is separate (written for the Business Bot, not the owner).

**Price disclosure policies (only two):**
- `relevant` — mention approved prices when useful; do not dump prices into unrelated answers; volunteering a price in every general description is not required.
- `on_request` — mention prices only when the customer asks about pricing/cost/quote/total (or a clear pricing follow-up such as “And how much?”). A general description or booking request alone does not trigger prices. A prior pricing answer does **not** permanently unlock disclosure for later unrelated questions.

**Demo scope (prompt):** the customer bot may answer from approved facts, clarify needs, collect listed intake in chat, and suggest the customer message the owner directly. It cannot book calendars, take payments, sync a CRM, or save/deliver an inquiry. An approved fact may describe a business process (for example that the owner confirms bookings), but that does not prove the bot saved details, sent a message, completed a handoff, or made a booking — those claims need an actual successful operation, which this demo does not perform. The system does **not** persist or deliver customer inquiries to the owner — only in-chat collect + redirect wording.

**Capability catalog (single source):** `CONFIG_CAPABILITIES` and `DEMO_SCOPE` in `profile.js` list every configurable field — what it represents and what it cannot represent — plus what the Business Bot can and cannot do. The Builder prompt renders both. Free-text fields (`tone`, `openingMessage`, FAQ answers, service notes, `ownerGoal`) accepting the words does not make a request supported: per-message scripts, formatting or emoji rules, fixed sign-offs, language rules, reminders, and other actions are unsupported.

**Configuration requests (prompt + enforced):** for every request in the owner's current message about how the bot behaves or what it does, whatever the topic, the Builder returns `configRequests: [{ request, status, field, alternative }]`. `request` must quote the owner's words; `status` is `supported`, `ambiguous`, or `unsupported`. Prompt: supported → update the field and describe it as proposed (live only after approval); ambiguous → ask one short question; unsupported → say so, offer the closest supported alternative by name, and wait for the owner to accept. Never say an unsupported request was added or noted. Accepting an offered alternative makes it a new supported request (`approved=false` that turn).
Enforced by `enforceConfigRequests` after each real Builder turn:
- A `supported` request that carries an `alternative` counts as unresolved.
- The field named by any unresolved request is restored to its previous draft value.
- If any request is unresolved, every setting field (tone, responseLength, priceDisclosure, assistantName, openingMessage) that changed this turn without a supported request naming it is restored too.
- If any request is unresolved, the turn is neither approved nor ready for review.
**Not enforced:** the model's classification itself. If it marks an unsupported request as `supported` with the right field, the change goes through, though it still needs review and approval. Invalid enums are still dropped by `reconcileDraft`.

## What it asks

Nothing in code is labeled mandatory, optional, or conditional except the intake gate below. The prompt tells the model to start with the business and desired outcome (not a feature checklist), help vague goals without inventing `ownerGoal`, absorb one-message dumps, allow mid-setup direction changes, and for unsupported requests clearly refuse and offer an in-scope alternative. It also discovers topics **when relevant** (partial information is fine):

- What the business does
- Owner goal / desired outcome for the bot (`ownerGoal`)
- Services and offerings
- Prices, if the owner has them, and how a price applies when that matters (per unit versus flat or per booking)
- Opening hours or availability
- Location or service area
- Common customer questions (FAQ: each entry is a customer question plus the owner’s answer; the Business Bot may use those answers as approved facts)
- Information the bot should collect from the customer (intake: name, phone, preferred day/time, and similar; the Business Bot asks for these in chat)
- Conversation settings when mentioned: tone, reply length, when to mention prices, assistant name, opening message (optional — defaults apply if the owner says nothing)

For a proposed opening, the prompt tells the model to check it against the draft facts, demo scope, and price setting. If the opening claims an unsupported capability (such as booking), states an unapproved fact or price, impersonates the owner or a human, or contradicts the price policy, the model should explain the conflict, keep the previous opening in the draft, skip review, and ask for a correction. This is prompt-only (no app check). In one focused run it worked for a booking-claim opening.

The prompt tells the model to keep FAQ items in `commonQuestions` as `{ question, answer }` (full answer text when the owner explained it — not topic titles alone) and intake items in `informationToCollect`, and not to mix them. It also tells the model to clarify ambiguities that would change a customer answer: pricing basis or units, discounts, what a service area covers, hours edge cases, cancellation or refund policy, and booking or availability rules. It says capacity or group size does not settle how price scales. Setting and capability requests follow the capability-honesty rules above (clarify / refuse+offer / propose-then-approve). Whether any of these questions actually appear depends on the model and the conversation. **Unverified:** that the model always stores the full owner answer in `commonQuestions[].answer` rather than a truncated paraphrase, always leaves `ownerGoal` null until clarified, always maps natural-language style/price-disclosure requests into the enum fields correctly, always refuses unsupported capabilities cleanly, always keeps proposed vs applied distinct in its wording, and which clarification questions it asks in a given interview.

Application checks (enforced):

1. **Approval minimum** — `hasEnoughForFirstBot` is true when there is a name or a description of what they do, and there is at least one service or that same description. A description alone can pass. A name with no description and no service cannot. Hours, location, prices, common questions, tone, responseLength, priceDisclosure, unknowns, `ownerGoal`, and a non-empty intake list are not required by this check.
2. **Intake answered before review** — if the model sets ready for review or approved while `informationToCollect` is not an array (still null / unanswered), the app refuses approval, does not append the approve summary, and appends a fixed question asking whether the customer bot should collect anything (and what, or none). Empty array is enough to pass this gate.
3. **Draft reconciliation** — after each Builder turn, `reconcileDraft` normalizes `responseLength` / `priceDisclosure` enums and preserves prior settled `informationToCollect`, `ownerGoal`, `tone`, `responseLength`, `priceDisclosure`, `assistantName`, and `openingMessage` when the model omits them, blanks them, or sends an invalid value (identity fields clear only via the explicit reset flags above). A style-only edit therefore cannot wipe an approved price-disclosure policy via a null draft field, and an opening-only edit cannot wipe the assistant name.

Seeded or hand-written profiles that omit `informationToCollect` are treated as nothing to collect when building the Flowise customer prompt; they do not go through the interview gate.

## Follow-ups and “enough”

The prompt tells the model to ask one or two questions at a time, adapt them to what was already said, and not repeat answered questions. The application does not count questions or pick the next one. It does send the current draft and a hint: either enough may already exist for a first bot, or basics are still missing, or intake is still unanswered.

The prompt tells the model to set ready for review once there is identity plus at least one offering or a clear description **and** `informationToCollect` is an array, then show a short summary of known facts (including `ownerGoal` when set and conversation settings when set) and explicit unknowns and ask the owner to approve, correct, or say “approve”. Code does not turn that hint into ready-for-review by itself, except for settings-only edits (below). Whenever a turn ends in review (the model set ready for review, the app forces one, or an approval was refused), the app appends the fixed `formatSummary` of the **current** draft, followed by a line telling the owner to reply **approve** or correct something. This happens even if the model wrote its own summary. The summary always includes an **Owner goal** line (`(not set)` when null) and a **Conversation settings** block (Tone, Reply length, Prices, Assistant name, Opening — with `(default)` / `(not set)` labels when using effective defaults). FAQ entries without an answer are shown as not recorded yet. When the profile is already approved and the pending draft differs from live fields (including effective conversation settings), the summary shows **Live approved (active customer bot)** and **Pending (not live until you approve)** blocks so draft edits are not mistaken for the active bot.

## Unknowns, contradictions, and corrections

The prompt tells the model not to invent facts (including not inventing `ownerGoal`). If the owner says something is undecided, unknown, or “not sure yet”, the model is told to put a clear statement in `unknowns` and keep the known parts in the normal fields. Null means a text field is unknown. The prompt tells it to notice contradictions and ambiguous spots gently.

On a correction or a later clarification, the prompt tells the model to update the draft immediately, drop resolved unknowns, show what changed, and ask for approval again. Code does not detect contradictions, unknowns, or corrections. On approval it copies non-empty text fields (including `ownerGoal`, `tone`, `assistantName`, `openingMessage`; identity fields also clear on an explicit reset flag) and non-null normalized behavior enums (`responseLength`, `priceDisclosure`), and replaces services, common questions, information to collect, and unknowns only when the draft includes those arrays. A null or blank text field does not clear a value already stored. An omitted array leaves the previous list in place. A null `informationToCollect` in the draft does not clear a previously settled array. A null or invalid behavior enum in the draft does not clear a previously settled setting (see reconcile + merge).

If the model reply is not valid JSON, the app keeps the previous draft and does not approve.

## Review, `/test`, `/end`, and later edits

The owner reviews the model’s message and, when the app appends it, the fixed summary. The prompt tells the model to set approved when the owner says “approve”, “looks good”, or “yes” after a summary of an already-pending draft — and **not** when “yes” is accepting an offered alternative that the model is applying in the same turn. **Application review gate (enforced, `applyReviewGate` in the session after every Builder turn):**
- When the app presents a review, it stores `reviewedDraftKey` on the profile: a hash of the rendered pending configuration.
- Approval is accepted only when an approval is requested and the stored key matches the current draft. An approval request is either a model `approved=true` or a short owner phrase: `approve`, `looks good`, `lgtm`, or `yes`/`ok`/`okay` with optional period (`shouldSoftApprove` documents the short-phrase path). The draft must also meet the identity/offering minimum, and intake must be answered.
- A changed draft alone is not enough. If no review of the current draft was presented, the app refuses, replies "Before I apply anything, please review the current version below.", and presents that review. The next approve then succeeds.
- Any later draft change produces a different key, so the earlier review no longer counts. In particular, a short “yes” that also introduces a new draft change this turn (e.g. accepting an offered setting alternative) keeps that new draft as pending and does **not** approve until a review of the new draft is presented and the owner approves again. When that happens, the app keeps the model’s pending-change message (it does not replace it with the stock “please review” note) and still appends the Live/Pending summary.
- The gate always keeps the turn’s draft (so accepting an alternative can update the pending configuration). Soft-approve only fires when that draft was already reviewed. A short approve of an unchanged pending draft that was never reviewed still gets the stock “please review” note.
- After approval the key still matches, so saying **approve** again re-syncs the same configuration (sync retry).
- **Settings-only edits:** when the business is already approved and the draft changes only conversation settings or identity (business facts equal), the app presents the review even if the model asked an unrelated question instead. Missing `ownerGoal` does not block the preview.
- If intake is still unanswered, the intake question is shown instead of a review.

`/test`, `/end`, and `/reset` are exact chat commands handled by the app, not the model. `/test` needs `approvedAt`, a chatflow id, and `flowiseSyncOk` (set only after Flowise create/update succeeds for that approval). It switches to TEST mode, starts a new customer conversation, and sends messages to Flowise. TEST chat does not change the approved profile. `/end` switches back to BUILD. For an approved bot it says the owner is back in editing mode and shows the editing menu (below). Before anything is approved it shows a short "still in first-time setup" line with no menu. It does not clear the approved profile. `/reset` wipes the saved profile and the chats and shows the opening line again.

## Editing menu (app-enforced, `src/ownerNav.js`)

Applies only in BUILD mode and only once the profile has `approvedAt`. First-time setup stays the guided interview: before approval, menu words and numbers go to the Builder model as normal messages.

- **Menu:** six options, rendered from fixed templates (no LLM call): 1 business details, 2 assistant role and information to collect, 3 questions and answers, 4 conversation style and price disclosure, 5 assistant name and opening message, 6 try as a customer. It ends with a line saying the owner can pick a number or just write what to change.
- **When it is shown:** after an approval whose Flowise sync succeeded in that turn (the model's message, then "Bot updated ✅", editing mode, and the menu), after the background sync worker succeeds (see Flowise sync), after `/end`, and when the owner sends a short menu request (whitelist: `menu`, `/menu`, `main menu`, `show menu`, `back to menu`, `options`, `תפריט`, `הצג תפריט`, `תפריט ראשי`, `חזרה לתפריט`, and close variants). Longer phrasings go to the Builder model. Ordinary Builder replies and review summaries do not repeat the menu. A pending sync (Flowise down) keeps the existing "waking up" note and shows no success message and no menu.
- **Numbers:** a standalone `1`–`6` is a menu choice only when the latest assistant entry in the Builder history is a menu (the entry carries `nav: 'menu'`; Supabase `build_history` is jsonb, so the marker persists). After any other reply (a section view, a Builder question), a number is passed to the Builder model as an answer.
- **1–5:** show the current values of that section's existing fields, then ask what to change. Section 1: name, what the business does, services, hours, location, unknowns. Section 2: `ownerGoal`, `informationToCollect`. Section 3: `commonQuestions`. Section 4: tone, reply length, price disclosure (defaults marked as default). Section 5: assistant name, opening message. If an unapproved draft changes that section, the view shows the live values and the pending values separately.
- **6:** the same code path as `/test`, with the same prerequisites, the same refusal replies, and a fresh test session.
- **Read-only:** showing the menu or a section never saves the profile, so it does not change the draft, does not invalidate `reviewedDraftKey`, and approves nothing. Navigation turns are appended to the Builder history so a follow-up like "make it warmer" has context. Free-text edits work at any time, including right after a section view of a different topic. Edits still go through the draft, review, and approval gate above.
- **Language:** menu, section views, the success line, and the return-to-editing line use Hebrew or English. The language comes from the newest owner message in the Builder history that has letters, skipping commands, short approve words, menu requests, and bare numbers. More Hebrew letters than Latin letters means Hebrew; otherwise English, which is also the fallback. The business name and opening message are never used. Other languages get English. Stored free-text values (tone, FAQ, services) are shown as stored. The app's review summary, the TEST-mode entry line, and sync error notes are still English.
- **Not verified:** that the model's own approval text never contradicts the app's sync result. For example, it may say the bot is updated even when the sync ends up pending.

A later edit in BUILD is another interview turn. The prompt tells the model to update the draft, show the change, and leave it unapproved until the owner confirms. The pending draft is saved immediately; the live fields and the Flowise bot stay as last approved until a later turn is marked approved and syncs. Behavior-only edits (tone / length / price disclosure / assistant name / opening) follow the same draft → approve → Flowise path and must not change business facts. The prompt tells the model to preview such an edit and ask for approval. The app also enforces the preview through the settings-only rule of the review gate. In the 2026-10-24 focused run, the model's own text still asked an unrelated follow-up question, and the app-rendered Live/Pending review was appended below it.

**Customer prompt structure (enforced by `buildCustomerSystemPrompt`):** APPROVED BUSINESS FACTS, BOT OBJECTIVE, CONVERSATION SETTINGS (tone, length, price policy, assistant identity / opening — with a priority statement that settings never establish facts/capabilities/intake), then BEHAVIOR RULES (closed-world contract wins on conflict). Approved identity and opening live inside conversation settings, separate from business facts.

**Flowise sync (enforced):** On approval the app pings Flowise (short timeout) and tries create/update. If Flowise is up, sync runs in that turn and `flowiseSyncOk` becomes true. If Flowise is down, cold-starting, or the sync errors (e.g. Render 502 HTML), the app still saves approved facts locally, sets `flowiseSyncPending` + `flowiseSyncRequestedAt`, kicks a wake ping, and tells the owner sync usually takes 1–2 minutes — it does **not** put full error HTML into the chat history. A background Flowise sync worker (same process gate as the WhatsApp inbound worker) polls pending profiles, retries sync when ping succeeds, then WhatsApp-notifies the owner and appends the note to Builder history. It checks the sender's current mode first. In BUILD, the note is "Bot updated ✅", editing mode, and the editing menu, which turns on menu navigation. In TEST, the note is "Bot updated ✅" plus a reminder that messages still go to the customer bot and `/end` returns to editing. It has no menu and no menu context. Owner-facing success notes never include Flowise names or chatflow ids; those go only to logs. If still down after ~5 minutes (`FLOWISE_SYNC_MAX_WAIT_MS`), it notifies once that sync gave up and clears `flowiseSyncPending`; the owner can say **approve** again later. While pending, `/test` replies that sync is still in progress. Flowise error strings stored in replies are truncated so they cannot blow the next Builder context window.

**Browser / local:** profile file on disk; session transcript in memory (file-backed sessions). **WhatsApp:** each sender has isolated session + profile + inbound queue in Supabase (`easybot_*` tables). The Twilio webhook validates `X-Twilio-Signature`, enqueues by `MessageSid`, ACKs quickly, and a durable worker processes messages in order per sender. **Unverified:** that the model always waits for confirmation, preserves every unknown, stores full FAQ answers (not truncated paraphrases), leaves `ownerGoal` unset until clarified, splits FAQ vs intake correctly without the gate, maps style/price-disclosure natural language reliably, or treats “yes” only as approval after a summary.
