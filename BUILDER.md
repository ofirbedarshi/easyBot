# How the Builder works

Describes the Builder as implemented on 2026-10-25. Questioning is model-driven, except for a short list of **required topics** the application asks itself (see "Required topics" below). Prompt text tells the model what to do; it does not guarantee the model will do it. Only the application checks below are enforced.

## What it does and produces

The Builder interviews the business owner in chat and fills a draft business profile. The first owner message in an empty BUILD chat only wakes the bot: the app discards that text and replies with a fixed welcome (product intro + invite to describe the business). The next message starts the interview. Later replies come from the model (`OPENAI_MODEL`, otherwise `gpt-4o-mini`). Localhost `/api/start` leaves the chat empty until that wake.

The draft can hold: business name, what the business does, **ownerGoal** (the bot goal: `answer_questions` | `collect_details` | `answer_and_collect` | null) plus **ownerGoalRequested** (the owner's own words, kept for demo research only), services (name, price, notes), hours, location or service area, common customer questions (FAQ), information the bot should collect from the customer (intake), explicit unknowns, and **conversation settings** (behavior — not business facts): **tone** (free text), **responseLength** (`short` | `standard` | null), **priceDisclosure** (`relevant` | `on_request` | null), and **assistant identity**: **assistantName** (free text | null) and **openingMessage** (owner's opening text | null). Unknown text fields are stored as null. `ownerGoal` stays null until the owner answers the goal question — the app never invents a goal. Any other stored value (including legacy free-text goals) is normalized to null on load, merge, and use, so it is never shown or sent to the Business Bot. `ownerGoalRequested` is copied on approval but never reaches the Business Bot or the review. `commonQuestions` is an array of `{ question, answer }` objects: `question` is what customers ask; `answer` is the owner’s explanation the Business Bot may tell customers. Legacy bare strings are normalized on load/merge to `{ question, answer: "" }`. Only FAQ items with a non-empty `answer` are injected into the Flowise customer prompt as approved facts. `informationToCollect` uses a different convention: **null** means the owner has not been asked yet; **[]** means the owner said collect nothing; a non-empty array lists the items. Each turn saves that draft. Live approved fields change only when the model marks the turn approved and the minimum below is met. Approval writes the profile (with `approvedAt`) and syncs a Flowise chatflow whose customer prompt is built from those approved fields: approved business facts (identity, services, hours, location, FAQ answers), explicit unknowns, intake whitelist, `ownerGoal` as one fixed BOT OBJECTIVE sentence per value (`OBJECTIVE_SENTENCES` in `customerPrompt.js`; conversational direction only, collecting is limited to the intake list), and — separately — conversation settings (tone, reply length, price disclosure). There is no approved language field; the Business Bot prompt tells the model to match the customer’s language as a behavior setting.

**Conversation settings defaults (enforced on read/use, not written into stored nulls):** when `tone` is null, effective tone is `friendly, clear, and professional`. When `responseLength` is null, effective value is `standard`. When `priceDisclosure` is null, effective value is `relevant`. Invalid enum spellings are normalized or dropped to null on load. Legacy profiles without the new keys load safely and keep any existing `tone`. When `assistantName` is null the Business Bot has no name (the app never invents one). When `openingMessage` is null the customer prompt tells the Business Bot to give a short introduction as the digital assistant of the approved business name (or "this business"). There is no fixed default opening text.

**Assistant identity (prompt, not enforced):** the customer prompt tells the Business Bot to say openly that it is a digital assistant and never to present itself as the owner or a human. It uses the opening only in its first reply of a **fresh conversation**, meaning a Flowise `sessionId` with no earlier turns in its Buffer Memory. Each `/test` starts a new `testSessionId`, and probes that pass their own session id are also fresh. If the first message is a real question, the Business Bot should open and answer it in the same reply, and should not re-introduce itself later. The app does not inject the opening; this is model behavior driven by chat history. The opening is customer-facing wording only. It cannot establish facts, prices, capabilities, or intake, and it does not override the price policy. Conflicting parts are to be left out.

**Explicit identity reset (enforced on merge):** an omitted, null, or blank `assistantName` / `openingMessage` in the draft preserves the live value. To clear one back to its default, the draft carries `assistantNameReset: true` / `openingMessageReset: true` with a null value. `reconcileDraft` keeps a pending reset flag across later turns until a new non-empty value replaces it. `mergeDraft` applies the reset on approval. The prompt tells the model when to set these flags (an owner asking to remove the name or restore the default opening); whether the model does so reliably is **unverified**.

**Price disclosure / reply-length labels (single source):** owner-facing wording for the enums lives in `PRICE_DISCLOSURE_LABELS` and `RESPONSE_LENGTH_LABELS` in `profile.js` (`relevant` → “when useful and relevant”; `on_request` → “only when the customer asks about pricing”; `short` / `standard` as themselves). `formatSummary` and the Builder system prompt both read those maps so the explanation, pending draft, and approval summary stay aligned. Customer-prompt policy text in `customerPrompt.js` is separate (written for the Business Bot, not the owner).

**Price disclosure policies (only two):**
- `relevant` — mention approved prices when useful; do not dump prices into unrelated answers; volunteering a price in every general description is not required.
- `on_request` — mention prices only when the customer asks about pricing/cost/quote/total (or a clear pricing follow-up such as “And how much?”). A general description or booking request alone does not trigger prices. A prior pricing answer does **not** permanently unlock disclosure for later unrelated questions.

**Demo scope (prompt):** the customer bot may answer from approved facts, clarify needs, collect listed intake in chat, and suggest the customer message the owner directly. It cannot book calendars, take payments, sync a CRM, or save/deliver an inquiry. An approved fact may describe a business process (for example that the owner confirms bookings), but that does not prove the bot saved details, sent a message, completed a handoff, or made a booking — those claims need an actual successful operation, which this demo does not perform. The system does **not** persist or deliver customer inquiries to the owner — only in-chat collect + redirect wording.

**Capability catalog (single source):** `CONFIG_CAPABILITIES` and `DEMO_SCOPE` in `profile.js` list every configurable field — what it represents and what it cannot represent — plus what the Business Bot can and cannot do. The Builder prompt renders both. Free-text fields (`tone`, `openingMessage`, FAQ answers, service notes) accepting the words does not make a request supported: per-message scripts, formatting or emoji rules, fixed sign-offs, language rules, reminders, and other actions are unsupported.

**Configuration requests (prompt + enforced):** for every request in the owner's current message about how the bot behaves or what it does, whatever the topic, the Builder returns `configRequests: [{ request, status, field, alternative }]`. `request` must quote the owner's words; `status` is `supported`, `ambiguous`, or `unsupported`. Prompt: supported → update the field and describe it as proposed (live only after approval); ambiguous → ask one short question; unsupported → say so, offer the closest supported alternative by name, and wait for the owner to accept. Never say an unsupported request was added or noted. Accepting an offered alternative makes it a new supported request (`approved=false` that turn).
Enforced by `enforceConfigRequests` after each real Builder turn:
- A `supported` request that carries an `alternative` counts as unresolved.
- The field named by any unresolved request is restored to its previous draft value.
- If any request is unresolved, every setting field (tone, responseLength, priceDisclosure, assistantName, openingMessage) that changed this turn without a supported request naming it is restored too.
- If any request is unresolved, the turn is neither approved nor ready for review.
**Not enforced:** the model's classification itself. If it marks an unsupported request as `supported` with the right field, the change goes through, though it still needs review and approval. Invalid enums are still dropped by `reconcileDraft`.

## What it asks

Nothing in code is labeled mandatory, optional, or conditional except the required topics and the approval-minimum name rule below. The prompt tells the model to start with the business only (invite them to tell about the business — not a feature checklist), ask exactly one clarifying question per turn, defer `ownerGoal` until `draft.name` is set, help vague goals without inventing `ownerGoal`, absorb one-message dumps, allow mid-setup direction changes, and for unsupported requests clearly refuse and offer an in-scope alternative. It also discovers topics **when relevant** (partial information is fine):

- Business name (required for a first bot — the model must not invent it)
- What the business does
- Owner goal for the bot (`ownerGoal`) — only after name is set
- Services and offerings
- Prices, if the owner has them, and how a price applies when that matters (per unit versus flat or per booking)
- Opening hours or availability
- Location or service area
- Common customer questions (FAQ: each entry is a customer question plus the owner’s answer; the Business Bot may use those answers as approved facts) — **required topic**
- Information the bot should collect from the customer (intake: name, phone, preferred day/time, and similar; the Business Bot asks for these in chat) — **required topic**
- Conversation settings when mentioned: tone, reply length, when to mention prices, assistant name, opening message (optional — defaults apply if the owner says nothing)

**Owner goal question (prompt only).** Once the name is set and `ownerGoal` is null, the prompt gives the model a fixed question (`OWNER_GOAL_QUESTION` in `builder.js`): "What's the main thing you'd like your bot to do for your business?", with examples (answer customer questions, help increase sales, guide customers toward booking an appointment, collect contact details) and an invitation to answer freely. The model must not phrase it as "outcome" or "customer bot". The context hint also reminds the model to ask it next while `ownerGoal` is unset, unless the owner is mid-correction or changing a setting.

**Bot goal mapping.** The Business Bot supports only two things: answering questions and collecting the listed details. So `ownerGoal` holds one of three values (`OWNER_GOAL_VALUES` / `OWNER_GOAL_LABELS` in `profile.js`): `answer_questions`, `collect_details`, `answer_and_collect`.
- Prompt: the model copies the owner's words into `ownerGoalRequested` and maps the answer to a value. A fully supported answer gets the matching value, `goalSupported=true`, and a one-sentence acknowledgment. Anything else (booking, sales, payments, follow-ups, or a mix that includes one of them) gets `answer_and_collect`, `goalSupported=false`, and a short `goalUnsupportedPart` (e.g. "book sessions").
- Enforced (`applyGoalSupport` in `builder.js`, part of `applyReviewGate`): a goal is unsupported when the model says `goalSupported=false`, or when the goal was set or changed this turn and the owner's message matches booking / sales / payments / reminders wording. In that case the app stores `answer_and_collect`. It also replaces the model's text before its next question with a fixed disclaimer: "Noted! {part} isn't available yet, so I'll set the bot to answer questions and collect details for now." The disclaimer appears only on that turn, never in the review.
- `reconcileDraft` keeps the previous value when the model sends an invalid or empty goal. `ownerGoal` is not required for review.
- **Unverified:** that the model maps supported wording to the right one of the three values, and that it flags unsupported goals that the keyword check misses.

For a proposed opening, the prompt tells the model to check it against the draft facts, demo scope, and price setting. If the opening claims an unsupported capability (such as booking), states an unapproved fact or price, impersonates the owner or a human, or contradicts the price policy, the model should explain the conflict, keep the previous opening in the draft, skip review, and ask for a correction. This is prompt-only (no app check). In one focused run it worked for a booking-claim opening.

The prompt tells the model to keep FAQ items in `commonQuestions` as `{ question, answer }` (full answer text when the owner explained it — not topic titles alone) and intake items in `informationToCollect`, and not to mix them. It also tells the model to clarify ambiguities that would change a customer answer: pricing basis or units, discounts, what a service area covers, hours edge cases, cancellation or refund policy, and booking or availability rules. It says capacity or group size does not settle how price scales. Setting and capability requests follow the capability-honesty rules above (clarify / refuse+offer / propose-then-approve). Whether any of these questions actually appear depends on the model and the conversation. **Unverified:** that the model always stores the full owner answer in `commonQuestions[].answer` rather than a truncated paraphrase, always leaves `ownerGoal` null until clarified, always maps natural-language style/price-disclosure requests into the enum fields correctly, always refuses unsupported capabilities cleanly, always keeps proposed vs applied distinct in its wording, and which clarification questions it asks in a given interview.

Application checks (enforced):

1. **Approval minimum** — `hasEnoughForFirstBot` is true when there is a non-empty business **name**, and there is at least one service or a description of what they do. A description alone cannot pass. A name with no description and no service cannot. Hours, location, prices, common questions, tone, responseLength, priceDisclosure, unknowns, `ownerGoal`, and a non-empty intake list are not required by this check.
2. **Required topics before the first review** (`REQUIRED_TOPICS` in `src/builderTopics.js`, checked in `applyReviewGate`). The list, in asking order:
   - `commonQuestions`: fixed question "What questions do customers usually ask you, and what do you answer? Share a few in your own words — or say skip if you prefer not to now." It is **skippable**: it counts as settled once the draft has at least one FAQ entry with an answer, or once the app has asked it and the owner has replied (anything, including "skip"). The app asks it once.
   - `informationToCollect`: the fixed intake question ("Before we finalize: should the customer bot collect any information..."). It is **not skippable**: it is settled only when `informationToCollect` is an array (`[]` for none). If the owner's reply leaves it null, the app asks again.

   When a turn would present a review or approve (model `readyForReview` / `approved`, or a short approve phrase once there is enough for a first bot) and a topic is still open, the app does not review or approve. It keeps the first non-question acknowledgment sentence of the model's text, appends that topic's fixed question, and records the topic in `draft.askedTopics` (app-owned; `reconcileDraft` carries it over and the model never sets it). One topic per turn.
   If the model asks an open topic in its own words (it reports `askingTopic`, and for FAQ the app also matches the wording of the model's question sentences), the app keeps the model's text up to its first question, replaces the rest with the fixed question, and records it as asked. This prevents asking twice when the owner skips. On the turn after a fixed question, the model gets a hint telling it how to store the answer.
   Required topics apply only before the first approval. An approved profile is never blocked by them. To make the Builder ask a new topic, add an entry to `REQUIRED_TOPICS`.
   The fixed questions are English only. **Unverified:** that the model always sets `askingTopic`. If it asks FAQ in wording the matcher misses and the owner skips, the app may ask FAQ once more.
3. **Draft reconciliation** — after each Builder turn, `reconcileDraft` normalizes `ownerGoal` / `responseLength` / `priceDisclosure` enums and preserves prior settled `informationToCollect`, `ownerGoal`, `tone`, `responseLength`, `priceDisclosure`, `assistantName`, and `openingMessage` when the model omits them, blanks them, or sends an invalid value (identity fields clear only via the explicit reset flags above). A style-only edit therefore cannot wipe an approved price-disclosure policy via a null draft field, and an opening-only edit cannot wipe the assistant name.

Seeded or hand-written profiles that omit `informationToCollect` are treated as nothing to collect when building the Flowise customer prompt; they do not go through the interview gate.

## Follow-ups and “enough”

The prompt tells the model to ask exactly one clarifying question per turn, adapt it to what was already said, not repeat answered questions, and not bundle two asks in one message. The application does not count questions or pick the next one (**unverified** that the model always asks only one). It does send the current draft and a hint: business name still required, enough may already exist for a first bot, or basics still missing (name plus offering/description). Right after a fixed required-topic question, it also sends how to store that answer.

The prompt tells the model to set ready for review once there is a non-empty business name plus at least one offering or a clear description, even if required topics are still open (the app asks them first), and to reply with **one short acknowledgment sentence only** — no summary, no questions, no bullet list, no "ready for review" (the app shows those). Code does not turn that hint into ready-for-review by itself, except for settings-only edits (below). **Review presentation (enforced, `renderReview` in `ownerNav.js`):** whenever a turn ends in review (the model set ready for review, the app forces one, or an approval was refused), the reply is:
1. The model's acknowledgment cut down by `reviewAck` to its first sentence that is not a question, a bullet/numbered line, a line ending in `:`, or review filler (review / summary / approve / set up / ready / prepare / below). It may be empty. The stock "please review" note is kept as is.
2. **First-time or unchanged:** "Here's your bot for {name}:" then one `Label: value` line per item (`summaryLines` in `ownerNav.js`), in editing-menu order: Business, Services (name and price, no notes), Area, Hours, Not decided (only when there are unknowns), Bot goal (the label of the stored value), Asks customers for, Q&A (count of answered entries), Style (tone / reply length / price setting, or "default"), Name & opening (or "default"). Values are cut at about 80 characters; full values are in the editing menu sections. Gaps are shown: empty area or hours read "not set (bot refers customers to you)", and empty Q&A reads "none yet (bot refers customers to you)". No markdown bold.
3. **Already approved with a pending draft:** "Here's what will change:" then only the changed lines in the same format, each with `(was: old)`. If the change is hidden by the short summary (e.g. a service note or a FAQ answer), the line ends with `(updated)` instead. Unchanged lines are not repeated.
4. "What would you like to do next?", then two options: **1. Try my bot** and **2. Make changes**, then "(or just tell me what to change 🙂)" The history entry carries `nav: 'review'`.

Hebrew and English follow `ownerLanguage`. The full field menu is not shown in the review. The fuller `formatSummary` (Live/Pending blocks, defaults labeled) remains for UI state and tests and is not pasted into chat.

**Prices (prompt only, not enforced):** the prompt tells the model to store each `services[].price` with its currency and unit as the owner said them (e.g. "200 ILS per 60-minute lesson"), never invent either, ask once when a bare number is unclear, and put the missing part in `unknowns` if the owner doesn't know. The review shows the price text exactly as stored; the app does not add or check currency/unit.

## Unknowns, contradictions, and corrections

The prompt tells the model not to invent facts (including not inventing `ownerGoal`). If the owner says something is undecided, unknown, or “not sure yet”, the model is told to put a clear statement in `unknowns` and keep the known parts in the normal fields. Null means a text field is unknown. The prompt tells it to notice contradictions and ambiguous spots gently.

On a correction or a later clarification, the prompt tells the model to update the draft immediately, drop resolved unknowns, set ready for review, and reply with a one-sentence acknowledgment only (the app shows the review and the two options). Code does not detect contradictions, unknowns, or corrections. On approval it copies a valid `ownerGoal` value, a non-empty `ownerGoalRequested`, and non-empty text fields (including `tone`, `assistantName`, `openingMessage`; identity fields also clear on an explicit reset flag) and non-null normalized behavior enums (`responseLength`, `priceDisclosure`), and replaces services, common questions, information to collect, and unknowns only when the draft includes those arrays. A null or blank text field does not clear a value already stored. An omitted array leaves the previous list in place. A null `informationToCollect` in the draft does not clear a previously settled array. A null or invalid behavior enum in the draft does not clear a previously settled setting (see reconcile + merge).

If the model reply is not valid JSON, the app keeps the previous draft and does not approve.

## Review, `/test`, `/end`, and later edits

The owner reads the acknowledgment, the summary lines, and the two options (Try my bot / Make changes). The prompt tells the model to set approved when the owner says “approve”, “looks good”, or “yes” after a presented review of an already-pending draft — and **not** when “yes” is accepting an offered alternative that the model is applying in the same turn. **Application review gate (enforced, `applyReviewGate` in the session after every Builder turn):**
- When the app presents a review, it stores `reviewedDraftKey` on the profile: a hash of the rendered pending configuration (same fingerprint as before, from the full facts block).
- Approval is accepted only when an approval is requested and the stored key matches the current draft. An approval request is either a model `approved=true`, a short owner phrase (`approve`, `looks good`, `lgtm`, or `yes`/`ok`/`okay` with optional period — `shouldSoftApprove`), or **Try my bot** (review option 1, the phrase `Try my bot`, or editing-menu option 6) while a review is pending (`reviewPending`). The draft must also meet the identity/offering minimum, and intake must be answered.
- A changed draft alone is not enough. If no review of the current draft was presented, the app refuses, replies "Before I apply anything, please review the current version below.", and presents that review. The next approve or **Try my bot** then succeeds.
- Any later draft change produces a different key, so the earlier review no longer counts. In particular, a short “yes” that also introduces a new draft change this turn (e.g. accepting an offered setting alternative) keeps that new draft as pending and does **not** approve until a review of the new draft is presented and the owner approves again. When that happens, the app shows the cut-down acknowledgment (filler like "approve when ready" is dropped), the list of what will change, and the two options.
- The gate always keeps the turn’s draft (so accepting an alternative can update the pending configuration). Soft-approve only fires when that draft was already reviewed. A short approve of an unchanged pending draft that was never reviewed still gets the stock “please review” note plus the review and the two options.
- After approval the key still matches, so saying **approve** again re-syncs the same configuration (sync retry).
- **Settings-only edits:** when the business is already approved and the draft changes only conversation settings or identity (business facts equal), the app presents the review even if the model asked an unrelated question instead. Missing `ownerGoal` does not block the preview. The model's question text is stripped; the owner sees the acknowledgment, what will change, and the two options.
- If a required topic is still open (first-time setup), its fixed question is shown instead of a review.

`/test`, `/end`, and `/reset` are exact chat commands handled by the app, not the model. `/test` needs `approvedAt`, a chatflow id, and `flowiseSyncOk` (set only after Flowise create/update succeeds for that approval). It switches to TEST mode, starts a new customer conversation, and sends messages to Flowise. TEST chat does not change the approved profile. `/end` switches back to BUILD. For an approved bot it says the owner is back in editing mode and shows the editing menu (below). Before anything is approved it shows a short "still in first-time setup" line with no menu. It does not clear the approved profile. `/reset` wipes the saved profile and the chats; the next non-command message wakes the fixed welcome again.

## Editing menu (app-enforced, `src/ownerNav.js`)

Applies in BUILD mode. **Menu words** (`menu`, `/menu`, `תפריט`, …) require `approvedAt`.

- **Review options (every review, including before first approval):** right after a review (`nav: 'review'`), `1` or `Try my bot` (`לנסות את הבוט`) = **Try my bot**, and `2` or `Make changes` (`לעשות שינויים`) = **Make changes**. Anything else goes to the Builder model. **Make changes** replies "What would you like to change?" plus the full editing menu below (`nav: 'menu'`), saves nothing, and works before first approval too, so `1`–`6` then work during first-time setup. **Try my bot** behaves exactly like editing-menu option 6 below. Before any review, menu words and bare numbers still go to the Builder model as normal messages.

- **Menu:** six options, rendered from fixed templates (no LLM call): 1 Business, 2 Bot goal & customer details, 3 Q&A, 4 Style & prices, 5 Name & opening, 6 Try my bot (same labels and order as the review summary). It ends with a line saying the owner can pick a number or just write what to change.
- **When it is shown:** after the owner picks **Make changes** on a review; after an approval whose Flowise sync succeeded via typing **approve** (model message, then "Bot updated ✅", editing mode, and the menu); after the background sync worker succeeds (see Flowise sync); after `/end`; and when an approved owner sends a short menu request (whitelist: `menu`, `/menu`, `main menu`, `show menu`, `back to menu`, `options`, `תפריט`, `הצג תפריט`, `תפריט ראשי`, `חזרה לתפריט`, and close variants). Longer phrasings go to the Builder model. A pending sync (Flowise down) keeps the "waking up" note (says to choose Try my bot or type `/test` when ready) and shows no success menu until the worker finishes.
- **Numbers:** a standalone `1`–`6` is a menu choice only when the latest assistant entry in the Builder history is a menu (the entry carries `nav: 'menu'`; Supabase `build_history` is jsonb, so the marker persists). After any other reply (a section view, a Builder question), a number is passed to the Builder model as an answer.
- **1–5:** show the current values of that section's existing fields, then ask what to change. Before first approval they show the **merged draft**. After approval they show live values; if an unapproved draft changes that section, live and pending are shown separately. Section 1: name, what the business does, services, hours, location, unknowns. Section 2: bot goal (label of `ownerGoal`), `informationToCollect`. Section 3: `commonQuestions`. Section 4: tone, reply length, price disclosure (defaults marked as default). Section 5: assistant name, opening message.
- **6 / Try my bot:** if a review of the current draft is pending (`reviewPending`: matching `reviewedDraftKey`, intake answered, enough for a first bot, and either not yet approved or draft differs from live), the app **approves that draft**, syncs Flowise, and on success enters TEST ("Bot updated ✅" + the TEST-mode line). If sync is still pending, it stays in BUILD with the waking-up note. If no review is pending, it is the same path as `/test` (same prerequisites and refusal replies).
- **Read-only (1–5 and menu words):** showing the menu or a section never saves the profile, so it does not change the draft, does not invalidate `reviewedDraftKey`, and approves nothing. **Try my bot** with a pending review is the exception (it approves). Navigation turns are appended to the Builder history so a follow-up like "make it warmer" has context. Free-text edits work at any time, including right after a section view of a different topic. Edits still go through the draft, review, and approval gate above.
- **Language:** menu, section views, the review and its two options, the success line, and the return-to-editing line use Hebrew or English. The language comes from the newest owner message in the Builder history that has letters, skipping commands, short approve words, menu requests, and bare numbers. More Hebrew letters than Latin letters means Hebrew; otherwise English, which is also the fallback. The business name and opening message are never used. Other languages get English. Stored free-text values (tone, FAQ, services) are shown as stored. The TEST-mode entry line and sync error notes are still English.
- **Not verified:** that the model's own acknowledgment never contradicts the app's sync result. For example, it may sound finished even when the sync ends up pending.

A later edit in BUILD is another interview turn. The prompt tells the model to update the draft and leave it unapproved until the owner confirms (one-sentence acknowledgment; the app shows what will change plus the two options). The pending draft is saved immediately; the live fields and the Flowise bot stay as last approved until a later turn is marked approved (via **approve** or **Try my bot**) and syncs. Behavior-only edits (tone / length / price disclosure / assistant name / opening) follow the same draft → approve → Flowise path and must not change business facts. The app also enforces the preview through the settings-only rule of the review gate.

**Customer prompt structure (enforced by `buildCustomerSystemPrompt`):** APPROVED BUSINESS FACTS, BOT OBJECTIVE, CONVERSATION SETTINGS (tone, length, price policy, assistant identity / opening — with a priority statement that settings never establish facts/capabilities/intake), then BEHAVIOR RULES (closed-world contract wins on conflict). Approved identity and opening live inside conversation settings, separate from business facts.

**Flowise sync (enforced):** On approval (typing **approve**, or **Try my bot** with a pending review) the app pings Flowise (short timeout) and tries create/update. If Flowise is up, sync runs in that turn and `flowiseSyncOk` becomes true. Approval via **Try my bot** then enters TEST immediately. Approval via **approve** stays in BUILD and shows the editing menu. If Flowise is down, cold-starting, or the sync errors (e.g. Render 502 HTML), the app still saves approved facts locally, sets `flowiseSyncPending` + `flowiseSyncRequestedAt`, kicks a wake ping, and tells the owner sync usually takes 1–2 minutes (then choose Try my bot or `/test`) — it does **not** put full error HTML into the chat history. A background Flowise sync worker (same process gate as the WhatsApp inbound worker) polls pending profiles, retries sync when ping succeeds, then WhatsApp-notifies the owner and appends the note to Builder history. It checks the sender's current mode first. In BUILD, the note is "Bot updated ✅", editing mode, and the editing menu, which turns on menu navigation. In TEST, the note is "Bot updated ✅" plus a reminder that messages still go to the customer bot and `/end` returns to editing. It has no menu and no menu context. Owner-facing success notes never include Flowise names or chatflow ids; those go only to logs. If still down after ~5 minutes (`FLOWISE_SYNC_MAX_WAIT_MS`), it notifies once that sync gave up and clears `flowiseSyncPending`; the owner can say **approve** again later.
